# MORI — English narration

Target: about 4 minutes including screen actions; rehearse with a timer and keep the final video under 5 minutes. Read only the narration, not the screen directions. Use fictional symptoms. Show the app in French. If speech recognition fails, type the same sentence and continue.

## 1. The problem — face camera, then MORI opening screen

Hello, I’m Caroline, a French international student studying in Korea.

This project started with a problem I experienced myself. When I felt unwell in Korea, I faced two questions: Which clinic should I go to? And how could I explain my symptoms in Korean?

There are many different medical specialties. For someone unfamiliar with the healthcare system, choosing a clinic can be confusing. And finding a clinic does not solve the language barrier when you arrive.

My teammate handles development, and I bring the experience of the student we are designing for.

Together, we built MORI: find a clinic, and explain your symptoms in Korean.

## 2. Finding care — emergency / clinic screen → ear, nose and throat → Yangdeok clinic

For this prototype, we focus on international students at Handong University preparing for a non-emergency clinic visit in Pohang.

The first screen separates emergency help from finding a clinic. Emergency guidance is accessible without an account.

For a regular visit, I can explore medical specialties using pictures and labels. Here, I choose ear, nose and throat.

The directory uses a dated snapshot of official information from Korea’s Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service. It does not show live opening hours, so I should check with the clinic before visiting.

I select a clinic and tap “Prepare this visit.” I can continue as a guest.

## 3. Preparing my words — fictional French input

Now I’ll demonstrate with a fictional example.

I write in French: “J’ai mal à la gorge depuis deux jours.” This means, “I have had a sore throat for two days.”

MORI asks about my main symptom, when it started, allergies, and medicines. I can type, choose a quick answer, or use voice input when my device supports it.

I do not need to write a diary first, and optional details can stay empty.

When I finish, MORI saves my original words and asks for permission to send the selected health information for AI translation. After I agree, Gemini translates it into Korean.

## 4. The key moment — original-input check → clinician view → PDF

I check that my original information is correct. I am not asked to certify a Korean translation that I cannot read.

Then I tap “Show to clinician.”

This is the most important moment in MORI: I now have something I can show at the clinic.

The sheet brings together my chosen clinic, symptoms in Korean, the original text, and information about allergies and medicines. I can also choose to include saved personal information or emergency contacts.

I can show this screen or save a PDF. This short example fits on one page. Longer content may need additional pages.

MORI does not diagnose me or make a booking. The sheet clearly identifies the translation as AI-generated and not clinically verified.

## 5. Closing — completed sheet, then face camera

We have tested the core flow with fictional cases, including actual Gemini translation and PDF download. Our next step is to evaluate it with more international students and healthcare professionals.

Our focus is simple: help students move from “I don’t know where to go or what to say” to “I have chosen a clinic, and I have my symptoms ready to show.”

That is MORI. Thank you.
